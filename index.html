Two hazards, one surface

A subduction earthquake shakes a structure and then floods it. The tsunami arrives at a building the shaking has already weakened, so assessing the two separately understates the risk. This is the combined surface — failure probability across both demands at once, with the earthquake eroding the capacity the tsunami then has to overcome.

How Locus works

Locus is the mathematical term for the set of all points satisfying a condition — which is exactly what a fragility surface is. Eight stages get you there, from a model to a decision.

Stages three and five are where the method earns its keep: the cases are independent so they run in parallel, and the reliability search converges on a design point instead of brute-forcing the probability.

  1. 01

    Define

    A structural model, and a hazard. The hazard enters as an input parameter, so it can be swapped or varied like any other.

  2. 02

    Sweep

    The parameter space is discretised. Every combination of intensity and configuration becomes a case to solve.

  3. 03

    Distribute

    Cases are independent, so they run concurrently. This is the step that turns weeks of sequential analysis into hours.

  4. 04

    Analyse

    Nonlinear dynamic analysis drives each case through a suite of ground motions, giving the response history the limit state is checked against.

  5. 05

    Reliability

    The first-order reliability method iterates to the design point on the limit state surface and returns a failure probability at that intensity — without the full Monte Carlo the same answer would otherwise cost.

  6. 06

    Fit

    The reliability analysis returns a probability at each intensity, not a curve. A lognormal fitted through those points gives the continuous fragility relationship.

  7. 07

    Surface

    The fitted curves join across the parameter space into a surface. A probability now exists for configurations that were never explicitly run.

  8. 08

    Compare

    The index reduces a surface to one number, so different structures can be ranked against each other for retrofit.

The work

Why a tool, and why a surface

A code check tells you a building complies. It does not tell you what that building will do when the ground moves, or when water arrives, or when the next hazard nobody planned for arrives. Those are different questions, and answering them takes hundreds of nonlinear analyses per configuration — which is not something anyone does by hand.

So the answer had to be a tool, and the tool had to be general. The hazard is an input, not a built-in assumption, and the output is a surface rather than a curve: a probability exists across the whole parameter space, including for configurations nobody explicitly ran. A probability is something an owner, a city, or a transport agency can decide on. A pass or a fail is not.

Expertise

01

Locus, the multi-hazard assessment tool

Software that computes fragility surfaces with the hazard as an input variable rather than a built-in assumption. Cases run in parallel and the failure probability comes from a first-order reliability search rather than brute-force simulation, which is what makes sweeping a whole parameter space tractable.

  • Ph.D. dissertation — Integrated Framework for Multi-Hazard Resilience Assessment of Infrastructures (2023)
  • First-order reliability method (FORM) for efficient fragility computation
  • Parallelised nonlinear dynamic analysis pipeline in Python and MATLAB
  • Demonstrated across three independent cases: skewed RC bridges, mass-irregular frames, and tsunami loading
  • Funded by the U.S. DOT Pacific Southwest Region UTC

02

A comparable fragility index

Fragility results are hard to compare across structural systems, which leaves retrofit prioritisation to judgement. This derives a single quantitative index that makes them commensurable.

  • Indexing Seismic Fragility Curves of Skewed RC Bridges (12NCEE 2022)
  • Extended in the dissertation to compare vulnerability across structural systems
  • Further generalisation in progress

03

Tsunami loading and ASCE 7

Where the tool meets the standard: hydrodynamic, hydrostatic and debris loading applied probabilistically rather than as a single design case.

  • Probabilistic fragility under ASCE 7 tsunami loads (Ports 2022)
  • Wave and bathymetric parameter effects on induced forces (J. Building Engineering, 2026)
  • Practising at the firm whose president chairs the ASCE Tsunami Loads and Effects Subcommittee

04

Instrumentation and damage detection

What feeds the tool from the field: smart sensors on aging bridges, multi-sensor fusion, and deep-learning crack detection from images.

  • Long-term structural health monitoring of aging bridges, funded by Hawaii DOT
  • Multi-sensor fusion for non-destructive condition assessment (12NCEE 2022)

Amr Ghanem · The tool

Locus

A multi-hazard assessment tool. The hazard is an input, not a built-in assumption: Locus predicts how likely a structure is to fail under hazards acting in sequence, and returns a resilience index rather than a pass or a fail.

A locus is the set of all points satisfying a condition — which is exactly what a fragility surface is. The tool computes one.

What it gives you

  • Prediction, not just evaluation

    A fragility curve answers for the configuration you ran. A surface answers across the whole parameter space at once, so a structure nobody explicitly analysed still has a result — and a change to the design can be tested before it is drawn.

  • Resilience as a number you can rank

    The output is a resilience index, so two different structural systems become directly comparable. That is what turns a set of probabilities into a retrofit priority list.

  • Hazards assessed together, not one at a time

    The hazard is an input, not a hard-coded assumption. The water meets a frame the shaking has already weakened, so assessing earthquake and tsunami separately understates the risk. Earthquake and tsunami are the demonstrated cases, not the limits.

  • Fed by instrumentation and AI

    Assessment is only as good as what it knows about the structure as built. Deep-learning damage detection and multi-sensor fusion on instrumented bridges supply that condition data — the AI reads the structure, the physics computes the failure probability.

How Locus works

Reliability drives the search; nonlinear analysis answers it. Five steps, from a demand to a surface.

  1. 01

    Sweep

    The parameter space is discretised — every hazard intensity and configuration becomes a case.

  2. 02

    Distribute

    Cases are independent, so they run concurrently. Weeks of analysis become hours.

  3. 03

    Iterate

    The coupled loop runs for each case until the design point converges.

  4. 04

    Fit

    A lognormal fitted through those points gives a continuous fragility curve.

  5. 05

    Surface

    The fitted curves join into one continuous surface.

  6. 06

    Compare

    The index reduces a surface to one number, so structures can be ranked.

Locus couples reliability analysis to nonlinear structural analysis and extends the pair to sequential multi-hazard loading, driving the established FERUM and ZEUS-NL codes as its solvers.

The work

Why a tool, and why a surface

A code check tells you a building complies. It does not tell you what that building will do when the ground moves, or when water arrives, or when the next hazard nobody planned for arrives. Those are different questions, and answering them takes hundreds of nonlinear analyses per configuration — which is not something anyone does by hand.

So the answer had to be a tool, and the tool had to be general. The hazard is an input, not a built-in assumption, and the output is a surface rather than a curve: a probability exists across the whole parameter space, including for configurations nobody explicitly ran. A probability is something an owner, a city, or a transport agency can decide on. A pass or a fail is not.

Amr Ghanem · Biography

About

A structural engineer and researcher. The through-line of the work is Locus, a multi-hazard assessment tool that computes how a structure fails under load, and returns a probability rather than a pass or a fail.

Two things separate it from standard practice. The first is the shape of the answer. A fragility curve gives failure probability against one intensity measure, for one configuration. Locus returns a surface — probability across a whole parameter space at once, so a result exists for configurations nobody explicitly ran. A locus, in the mathematical sense, is the set of all points satisfying a condition; that is precisely what the surface is.

The second is that the hazard is a variable. Most fragility work is built around a particular hazard and has to be rebuilt for the next one. Here the hazard enters as an input alongside the structural parameters, which is what lets earthquake and tsunami be assessed together rather than separately. Those two are the cases it was demonstrated on, not the limits of it.

Building it meant answering the questions underneath it one at a time. What happens when a building's use changes and mass irregularity appears where nobody designed for it. How the angle of a bridge pier changes its seismic response. How seafloor bathymetry changes the tsunami force reaching a wall. Each is a published paper, and each was a test of whether the method held when the hazard changed.

Alongside it sits an index that makes fragility results comparable across different structural systems — which is what turns a set of probabilities into a retrofit priority. That work continues at UH, in parallel with structural design and analysis at Martin, Chock & Carden in Honolulu.

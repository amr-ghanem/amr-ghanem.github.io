Biography

About

I am a structural engineer and researcher working at the point where hazard science meets practice. My doctoral work at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa built an integrated framework for multi-hazard resilience assessment of infrastructure — fragility analysis for earthquakes and tsunamis, structural health monitoring with smart sensors, and deep-learning damage detection. I now design and analyse buildings, bridges and infrastructure at Martin, Chock & Carden in Honolulu.

What I care about is the gap between what a code check tells you and what a structure will actually do. Fragility analysis is how you close it. Most of my published work is some version of that question: what happens when a building's use changes and mass irregularity appears where nobody designed for it; how the angle of a bridge pier changes its seismic behaviour; how seafloor bathymetry changes the tsunami force reaching a building.

I also write code. Fragility pipelines, image-processing crack detection, Revit automation. Two of those ideas became competition-winning ventures at UH.
